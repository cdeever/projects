Sweetie's 2015 Honda CR-V got clipped sitting in her own driveway, and the driver's door came out of it dented, drafty, and noisy. Rather than hand it to a body shop and watch the insurance premium climb, we bought a color-matched salvage door off eBay and swapped it ourselves. Done, and you can't tell anything ever happened.

## The situation

Sweetie's CR-V was parked in the driveway at the wrong place at the wrong time. Crunch!

The original door still worked fine, but post-crunch it came with two new features. The first was a whirring sound at about 40 MPH. The second was complimentary air conditioning — in the wintertime. The dent was well past what a quick pull was going to fix.

## What we did

There were three ways to go about this.

**Option 1: body shop, probably through insurance.** The "easiest" option, except that higher insurance rates are destined to follow. It also meant being without the car for a stretch, or dealing with getting a rental.

**Option 2: reskin the door.** Cheap — if your welding and hammering skills are current, not to mention your car painting skills. A bit too advanced for DIY around here.

**Option 3: find a salvage door in the same color and swap it.** All unbolt and rebolt. Perfect for DIY.

Option 3 it was. The catch: despite the popularity of the 2015 CR-V, there is not an ample supply of black driver's side doors floating around. So I set up an eBay notification search, cast a few lines in the water, and waited for a complete door to come up at the right price. A few months later — finally, a door we could order.

I had it shipped to my house. The moment it arrived, I knew I should have shipped it straight to Sweetie's house, where we were going to do the work in her large garage. The door came nicely packed upright on a pallet, and the driver used his mobile forklift to set the whole package down in my garage. This was not going to fit in either car. Luckily we were able to borrow a trailer to load it up and redeliver it to "the shop."

We did the work in the lull between Christmas and New Year 2024, which was fitting — nothing sharpens your motivation to fix a drafty door like December.

The swap itself was mostly a drop-in replacement, but the new door didn't come complete. The mirror was missing, and the door lock had to come across too so Sweetie's key would still work. Once the old door was off, we transplanted both. The mirror was easy — a few bolts and a plug-in harness.

## What surprised me

The tricky part was a piece we're all familiar with but never actually think about: the mechanism that keeps the door from swinging freely and instead settles it in increments — one notch about halfway, another notch all the way open. It's called the door check, and I'd never once considered it until it was in my hands. It had to fit just right for the door to operate normally. After some finagling, we got it working the way it should.

## Result

After a few hours we were all set with the new door. It looked great, except for the salvage yard's paint-marker scribbling on the window and skin — you can see it in the photo up top. A good wash took care of that.

Because no paint was required, the door doesn't hint that anything ever happened. Good as new. I mean, good as used!

All said and done, the repair came in just under $1500 — about $1000 less than the body shop quotes, and without the insurance claim that would have followed us around for years.

## Takeaways

- If the damaged panel bolts on, a color-matched salvage part lets you skip bodywork and paint entirely — the two things that make this job a professional's job.
- Paint match is the whole game. Waiting months for the right color was cheaper and better looking than any amount of bodywork skill I don't have.
- Set up the saved search and be patient. The notifications did the hunting; I just had to be ready when the right listing appeared.
- Ship the part to where the work is going to happen. A car door arrives on a pallet by forklift, not in a back seat.
- Budget time for the small transplants. Hanging the door was the easy part — the mirror, the lock, and that door check were where the afternoon actually went.
- Saving $1000 against the shop quote was only part of it. The bigger win was never opening an insurance claim in the first place.
